# Sources: How cat's eyes clean themselves (#11, 28 Nov 2026)

Each claim has one primary/official source or two independent reputable ones. Quotes are the pages' own wording.

| Claim in the video | Sources |
| --- | --- |
| When a tyre squashes a cat's eye, the rubber closes over the glass like an eyelid and wipes it clean, using rainwater caught in its base | [Calderdale Museums: Percy Shaw](https://museums.calderdale.gov.uk/percy-shaw) ("a rubber cushion which collected rainwater and closed like an eyelid over the glass stud when a car tyre ran over it"; "Thus, the reflective stud was kept clean"); [Wikipedia: Cat's eye (road)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat%27s_eye_(road)) ("A fixed rubber wiper cleans the surface of the reflectors as they sink below the surface of the road (the base tends to hold water after a shower of rain…)"); [Institute of Making: Cat's Eyes](https://www.instituteofmaking.org.uk/materials-library/material/cats-eyes) ("the cast iron base collects rain water"; "the cat's eyes are pushed down into the rainwater, cleaning the glass beads"); [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2025/12/08/the-engineering-that-makes-a-road-cats-eye-self-cleaning) ("a rubber dome that deforms to both protect the reflectors and wipe them clean using any water that's pooled in the area below them"); [MyLearning](https://www.mylearning.org/resources/a-catseye-percy-shaws-life-saving-invention) ("collecting rainwater and then using this to wash the bulbs clean when a car drives over the catseye, compressing the rubber case") |

## Dropped or softened
- "Two pairs of reflectors, one pair facing each way": only Wikipedia says it, so it isn't spoken (the drawing just shows two lenses).
- "The glass sinks below the road surface": only Wikipedia says it in those words; the video says the rubber closes over the glass.
- "Voted one of Britain's top 10 design icons in 2006": only Wikipedia, so it's left out.
- Ministry of Transport backing (James Callaghan's committee) and "a million a year": Wikipedia only, so they're left out.
- Shuttered headlights in the blackout: Wikipedia only, so it isn't spoken.
- The tram-lines version of the origin story is left out; the cat story is introduced as "the most famous story", not as fact.
- Quiz options B (bus lane) and C (speed camera ahead) are deliberately wrong; Rule 132 gives green as slip roads and lay-bys.
