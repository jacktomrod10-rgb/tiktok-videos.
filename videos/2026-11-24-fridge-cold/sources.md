# Sources: Your fridge doesn't make cold (episode 5, short, 24 Nov 2026)

| Claim in the video | Source(s) |
| --- | --- |
| A fridge doesn't make cold; it moves heat from inside to outside | Penn State EGEE 102, 6.4 Refrigerators: https://courses.ems.psu.edu/egee102/node/2402 ("A refrigerator is a heat mover"); Danfoss: https://www.danfoss.com/en-gb/about-danfoss/our-businesses/cooling/the-fridge-how-it-works ("transfer heat from inside to outside") |
| The heat goes into the room; the back of the fridge feels warm | Penn State ("heat naturally flows out of the coils and into the room"; coils "on the back or bottom of your fridge"); Danfoss ("it feels warm if you put your hand on the back side of fridge near the metal pipes") |
| Refrigerant evaporates in the pipes inside and soaks up heat | Penn State ("flows through them and evaporates… steals that energy as heat from the air and food inside"); Danfoss (evaporator "located inside a refrigerator") |
| The compressor squeezes the gas, which makes it hotter | Penn State ("increasing its pressure and temperature dramatically"); Danfoss ("makes the refrigerant hot"); National Grid: https://www.nationalgrid.com/stories/energy-explained/how-do-heat-pumps-work |
| The back pipes release the heat, the gas turns back to liquid, and the cycle repeats | Penn State ("condenses back into a liquid, ready to repeat the cycle"); Danfoss ("the process starts all over again") |
| Air conditioners work the same way | Penn State ("similar to an air conditioner"); ENERGY STAR: https://www.energystar.gov/products/ask-the-experts/how-does-heat-pump-work (air conditioners "use a refrigerant to absorb unwanted heat") |
