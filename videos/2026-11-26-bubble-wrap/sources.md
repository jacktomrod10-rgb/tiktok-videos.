# Sources: Bubble wrap was invented as wallpaper (#09, 26 Nov 2026)

Each claim has one primary source or two independent reputable ones. The US Embassy page is a reprint of the
Smithsonian article, so it isn't counted as a second source.

| Claim in the video | Sources |
| --- | --- |
| In 1957 two inventors (Alfred Fielding and Marc Chavannes) sealed two shower curtains together, trapping air bubbles | [Wikipedia: Bubble Wrap (brand)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_Wrap_(brand)) ("invented in 1957 … sealed two shower curtains together"); [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/accidental-invention-bubble-wrap-180971325/) ("two pieces of plastic shower curtain through a heat-sealing machine … trapped air bubbles", 1957); [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2015/07/06/bubble-wrap-redesigned-pop-less-packaging-material-ibubble-sealed-air/amp/) ("invented by accident in 1957 … fusing together a pair of shower curtains") |
| They tried to sell it as textured wallpaper, and it didn't catch on | [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/accidental-invention-bubble-wrap-180971325/) ("trying to create a textured wallpaper in 1957"); [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2015/07/06/bubble-wrap-redesigned-pop-less-packaging-material-ibubble-sealed-air/amp/) ("textured wallpaper … didn't catch on"); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_Wrap_(brand)) ("unsuccessful as wallpaper"); [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/history/7-snappy-facts-about-bubble-wrap) ("never took off") |
| Next they pitched it as greenhouse insulation, which didn't take off either | [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/accidental-invention-bubble-wrap-180971325/) ("greenhouse insulation … about as successful as textured wallpaper"); [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/history/7-snappy-facts-about-bubble-wrap) ("hard to convince buyers"); [Invention & Technology](https://www.inventionandtech.com/content/bubble-wrap-wallpaper-stress-reliever) ("difficult to market plastic for walls") |
| IBM introduced the 1401 in 1959 | [IBM history: The IBM 1401](https://www.ibm.com/history/1401) ("introduced on October 5, 1959") (primary) |
| IBM needed a way to ship it without damage | [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/accidental-invention-bubble-wrap-180971325/) ("needed a way of protecting the delicate device during transit"); [Invention & Technology](https://www.inventionandtech.com/content/bubble-wrap-wallpaper-stress-reliever) (shipping the hardware "without damaging it") |
| Their company was Sealed Air; it pitched the bubbles to IBM as packaging | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_Wrap_(brand)) (Sealed Air founded by Fielding and Chavannes, 1960); [MMH](https://www.mmh.com/article/sealed_air_celebrates_55th_anniversary/Sealed_Air) (founded 1960 by Fielding and Chavannes); [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/history/7-snappy-facts-about-bubble-wrap) and [Invention & Technology](https://www.inventionandtech.com/content/bubble-wrap-wallpaper-stress-reliever) (Sealed Air marketer Frederick Bowers pitched it to IBM) |
| IBM used it to protect the 1401 during shipment | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_Wrap_(brand)) ("used the product to protect the IBM 1401 computer during shipment"); [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/accidental-invention-bubble-wrap-180971325/) ("They could ship their computers without damage") |
| The 1401 became by far the best-selling computer yet | [IBM history](https://www.ibm.com/history/1401) ("by far the best-selling computer up to that point") (primary) |
| In 2015 Sealed Air unveiled a version that doesn't pop (iBubble Wrap) | [Engadget, 3 Jul 2015](https://www.engadget.com/2015/07/03/bubble-wrap-has-no-pop/); [Fortune, 2 Jul 2015](https://fortune.com/2015/07/02/bubble-wrap-is-not-poppable/); [Dezeen, 6 Jul 2015](https://www.dezeen.com/2015/07/06/bubble-wrap-redesigned-pop-less-packaging-material-ibubble-sealed-air/amp/) |
| Its bubbles are linked in columns; squeeze one and the air flows into its neighbours, so it's much harder to burst | [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2015/07/06/bubble-wrap-redesigned-pop-less-packaging-material-ibubble-sealed-air/amp/) ("columns of interlinked bubbles that allow air to be transferred to neighbouring pods when squashed, making it harder to burst"); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2015/07/03/bubble-wrap-has-no-pop/) ("long, interconnected columns, so they won't pop under normal pressure"); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/trending/bubble-wrap-no-pop-1.3136333) ("the air will flow into any free space in adjacent bubbles") |
| It ships flat and is pumped full of air where it's used | [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2015/07/06/bubble-wrap-redesigned-pop-less-packaging-material-ibubble-sealed-air/amp/) ("transported deflated … pumped full of air on site"); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2015/07/02/bubble-wrap-is-not-poppable/) ("flat plastic sheets that can be filled with air via a custom pump"); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2015/07/03/bubble-wrap-has-no-pop/) |
| The company said the poppable original wasn't going anywhere | [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2015/07/06/bubble-wrap-redesigned-pop-less-packaging-material-ibubble-sealed-air/amp/) ("the original version you love to pop isn't going anywhere"); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/trending/bubble-wrap-no-pop-1.3136333) (company tweet: "original #BubbleWrap (with pop!) isn't going away") |

## Dropped (couldn't verify well enough)

- "1/50th of the space": Engadget, Fortune and Dezeen say one-fiftieth (via the WSJ), but CBC says one-fifth, so the figure is left out.
- "IBM was the first customer": only Wikipedia says "first client"; the video says IBM used it instead.
- "Enough is made each year to circle the equator 10 times": one undated Mental Floss line only.
