# Sources: What if you jumped into a hole through the Earth?

Assumes the classic physics thought experiment: a straight tunnel through the centre, no air resistance, and
ignoring heat (the video says so on screen).

| Claim | Source |
| --- | --- |
| About 38 minutes (38 min 11 s) with a realistic Earth; the old uniform-Earth answer was about 42 minutes | https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.1342 (Klotz, Am. J. Phys.) ; https://www.livescience.com/50312-how-long-to-fall-through-earth.html |
| Gravity gets stronger deeper down, to about 110% of the surface value, then weakens to zero at the centre | Klotz quoted in https://www.foxnews.com/science/how-long-to-fall-through-earth-quicker-than-thought ; https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.1342 |
| Top speed over 8 km per second | https://www.livescience.com/50312-how-long-to-fall-through-earth.html |
| Halfway, gravity switches direction; you have to grab the far end or fall back, back and forth like a pendulum | https://www.livescience.com/50312-how-long-to-fall-through-earth.html |
| Near the centre the core is about 6,000 °C, about as hot as the Sun's surface | https://www.esrf.fr/news/general/Earth-Center-Hotter ; https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna51663933 |
